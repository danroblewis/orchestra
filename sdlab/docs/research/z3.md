# Z3: identifiability proofs and exact structure search on noise-free data

**Status:** done (2026-10-21). Experiment A, the `sdlab-ladder ident` tool: adopt as the identifiability
instrument. Experiment B, the strategy `z3exact`: keep as a specialist (exact where a linear structure
fits; behind matrix overall).
**Code:** `sdlab/ladder/z3_ident.py` (the class and the search), `sdlab/ladder/z3_ident_report.py`
(the command and the comparison), `sdlab/discovery/strategies/z3exact/` (`core.py` is its own copy of
`z3_ident.py`, edited together with it). Commits: c291f87, 17c33ce, 738c764, 096d024, 206932c,
4d317d5, 4fb15c0 (tool and strategy), 299c229 (z3exact's best effort).
**Dependency:** `z3-solver` 5.3.1 (MIT). **Tests:** `tests/test_z3_ident.py`, `tests/test_z3exact.py`.

## The question

On a noise-free variant (none-short, none-long: the engine's levels rounded to 4 decimals), several
models can fit the data equally well; the scorer guesses this with its `equivalent` verdict and the
analysis (`sdlab-ladder analyse`) with its `unidentifiable` label. Can a solver *prove* it instead:
exactly one structure of a model class fits, or these N do?

## The model class (both experiments)

Each measured stock's one-step change is a sum of *terms*, each a number times a *column*: a measured
stock's level, a measured input (the step from t[i] to t[i+1] reads the levels at t[i] and the inputs
at t[i+1], as the shared matrix method aligns them), or a constant; with announced eras every column is
split per era (each era its own numbers). Announced injections are added at the start of their step.

- A **move** between two stocks of one unit is one number in two rows (what one loses the other gains;
  its sign gives the direction). Two move classes: `ends` (a move reads the level of one of its two
  stocks: SD's usual drain or transfer) and `any` (a move may read any column: every decomposition of a
  linear system).
- An **outside term** on stock s reading column j is allowed only where the boundary allows it. Two
  rules (`--boundary`): `kinds` (the default), on a declared side of s whose kinds hold the column's
  kind (`measured` for an input, `estimate` for a constant or a level), as the case writes it and
  matrix's boundary.py enforces it; `sides`, on any declared side, as the scorer judges it (stock and
  side only; some cases declare `estimate` for a flow that reads an input, e.g. 204's valve). With no
  boundary declared, everywhere. An outside flow on a one-sided stock is kept at or above 0 on every
  step.
- Terms on one stock's row reading one column are one term: a gap inflow (c - kA) and an inflow c plus
  a drain kA, or births and deaths of one stock, are the *same* structure here (no data can separate
  their numbers). The tool lists where the boundary would allow such a split ("same-row splits").
- Not in the class: rates that are not linear in the columns (products, floors that bind, MIN,
  effects, delays with hidden stages), unannounced changes, sub-steps. Data from such a truth fit
  nothing, and the verdict says "outside the class" rather than inventing a fit.

## Fitting within epsilon, exactly

The levels are parsed as the decimals they were written as (Fraction of their repr), so the changes
and inputs are exact rationals, and Z3 decides over the reals (linear real arithmetic, exact). The
data's rounding (d decimals, read from the data, h = half a unit of the last decimal) bounds the true
model's residual on a step of stock s by 2h + h * sum_j |A_sj| over the level columns (A_s: the true
net numbers of the row). The tolerance uses twice the least-squares row's sum in place of the true one
(a fixed number per stock, so the constraints stay linear), plus a float slack of 1e-9 of the largest
level. On none-short this is about 1e-4 per step.

## The search

A Boolean per term (included) and a real number per term (0 unless included). Structures are counted
*irreducible*: Z3 (Solver with an at-most-k cardinality constraint, k = 0, 1, ...) finds the smallest
fitting size k*, then enumerates: find one, block it and every superset of it (one clause), ask again,
until no other structure of size k* (and, with `--slack s`, k* + s) fits, or a cap (20) or the budget
(60 s a cell in the study). Verdict: **identifiable** (exactly one structure of size k*), **not
identifiable** (several, each printed with its least-squares numbers), **unknown (timeout)**, or
**outside the class** (nothing fits at any size).

How it is made fast without changing what fits (each found by a failure on the way, all in the commits):

1. **The data's steps stay out of Z3.** Z3 holds only the Booleans, the numbers, and the bounds every
   net number must lie in for its row to fit every step (two LPs per stock and column, HiGHS, columns
   scaled to 1, widened outward), with "some term must reach (s, j)" wherever a bound excludes 0. Z3's
   exact simplex on hundreds of rows of large rationals ignored its timeout (pool items of 1500 s);
   `push` propagates without one too, so the size limit is an assumption literal, not a push.
2. **Each candidate is verified on every step outside Z3**: a float LP (HiGHS) for its numbers, then an
   exact check in rationals; a structure that does not fit is excluded exactly (that set, not its
   supersets) and Z3 is asked again. An LP that fails numerically is "undecided", never "does not fit"
   (an unscaled LP on counters of 1e5 once made a linear case "outside the class").
3. **Nothing fits if all the terms together do not** (one LP): "outside the class" before any search.
4. Measured inputs are read to 9 significant digits (their rounding joins the tolerance), so the
   rationals stay small; a class of more than 600 candidate terms is not searched.

On the way: Z3 with every step as a constraint needed 34 s on case 302 and timed out on 305; with lazy
rows and the bounds, 0.1 s and 0.2 s; the final design (no steps in Z3) decides 305 in 0.2 s and stays
within its budget on every cell.

## Experiment A: identifiability proofs

`uv run sdlab-ladder ident CASE [--variant none-short] [--moves ends|any|ends,any] [--slack 1] [--hidden]`
(`--cases` for a sweep; above 40 cells it needs `--local` and is logged: the pool runs strategies, not
this tool). `--hidden`: where nothing fits, the necessary condition of one hidden linear stock (the
measured levels then obey a second-order linear recurrence; an LP per stock); no structure is
enumerated with a hidden stock.

**Run** (2026-10-21, commit 4fb15c0, single-threaded, here with `--local`: the pool runs strategies, not
this tool; logged in `~/.sdlab/local-runs.log`): the Tests set (296 cases, 280 with noise-free variants;
kubernetes 300-317 among them), `--slack 0 --budget 60`:

    uv run sdlab-ladder ident --cases <Tests> --variant none-short --moves ends --boundary sides --local
    uv run sdlab-ladder ident --cases <Tests> --variant none-short --moves any --budget 30 --local

(`<Tests>`: every case but tags real, realistic, particles, physics, heavy; the comparison counts below
come from the printed lines, the analysis labels from `analysis.get`, the scorer's verdicts from the

## Experiment B: z3exact

`sdlab/discovery/strategies/z3exact/`: the smallest structure of the class that fits every one-step
change within the data's rounding, with the boundary as hard constraints, its numbers refitted by least
squares (all stocks jointly), flows built from the terms (a move's sign gives its direction; constants
per era as `byTime`). The classes are tried in turn: ends moves with the boundary as written, then by
sides, then any moves; of several smallest structures the one with the least squared error is returned
(the note says how many tied). Exact classes get 25 s each, everything 95 s.

**When nothing fits** (noisy data, or a truth outside the class) it first returned no flows. That read,
rightly, as "it did not run" (the user, 2026-10-21: "it knows there are 3 stocks and it returns just
those 3 stocks with no relations between them"). It now returns a **best effort, labelled so** (the
note starts "z3exact BEST EFFORT ... not proved by Z3", and `result["z3"]["best_effort"]` is true):
backward elimination on the same class, each stock's squared error kept within 1.05x the all-term
model's, outside and cross-reading terms dropped first. Only a class over 600 candidate terms (317)
still returns nothing.

**Run** (2026-10-21, commit 299c229; the strategy's code is unchanged since): the Tests set's noise-free
cells, 560. Submitted to the pool (jobs 30, 131, 149, 158, 180, 184 at successive commits); the last,
job 184, sat behind a 1274-item p300 job with no slot for an hour (16 of 560 done, about 17 hours
estimated), so it was cancelled and the same cells were run here with `--local --jobs 4` (logged), 396 s
of wall time:

    uv run sdlab-ladder run --strategies z3exact --exclude-cases tag:real,tag:realistic,tag:particles,tag:physics \
        --variants none-short,none-long --every-variant --jobs 4 --local

2026-10-21; those are of mixed versions, and milp's job was still running: its none-long coverage is

## Verdict

**Experiment A: adopt as the identifiability instrument.** On noise-free data it replaces the guess
with a proof inside an explicit class, in a twentieth of a second for most cells: 168 of 280 none-short
cells decided inside the class (119 identifiable, 49 not, each rival structure printed), 103 proved
outside it (no linear structure of the class fits: a nonlinear truth, an unannounced change, a hidden
stock). It agrees with the analysis label on 122 of the 168; the disagreements are mostly by design
(33 same-row splits, which Z3 merges) or the analysis ignoring the boundary (4, e.g. 305), and in 5 Z3
finds an equal-size rival the analysis misses (047's goal gaps, where the scorer does hand out
`equivalent`). Where Z3 proves several structures, 80% of the cells have many `equivalent` verdicts;
where it proves one, 3%. Limits: the class is linear (no products, floors, MIN, effects, delays, hidden
stocks: those are only "outside the class", with a necessary-condition test for one hidden stock), the
tolerance assumes the true row's sum of |numbers| is at most twice the least-squares row's, the
verification LP is float (each answer is then checked exactly; a numerical failure is "undecided"),
and cases of 6+ stocks can time out (8 of 280 at 60 s; 317 is too large).

**Experiment B: keep as a specialist, not a main-line candidate.** Where an exact structure exists it
is as good as the best (114 of 119 provably identifiable cells pass, matrix 113; 131 of 173 cells with
an exact fit, matrix 122), and its answer comes with a proof of minimality. Elsewhere it has nothing:
the best effort passes none of the 107 cells outside the class (matrix 20), so overall it is behind
matrix (131 vs 142 none-short, p = 0.09), structures and milp. It is useless on noisy data by
construction (the best effort is a plain backward elimination). The ideas worth taking elsewhere: the
outside-the-class test (one LP) as a cheap "this case needs nonlinear terms" signal for a router-free
strategy, and the implied-bounds plus exact-verification loop for any exact search.

**Compared with milp** (HiGHS, MIN regimes, best-subset; docs/research/milp.md): milp passes 21 more
none-short cells, mostly where Z3 can only say "outside the class": its MIN regimes reach kubernetes
306, 307, 314, 315. On the linear cells both are exact searches over a similar class; z3exact's
difference is the proof (it enumerates every rival of the smallest size; milp returns one optimum).

**Open:** structures with one hidden stock (bilinear; only the necessary condition is tested); merging
era numbers that agree (z3exact pays a term per era: 157 passes but at 1.6x the truth's complexity); a
noise-aware tolerance (a measured standard error in place of the rounding) would make experiment A's
proofs speak to the counts variants too.
