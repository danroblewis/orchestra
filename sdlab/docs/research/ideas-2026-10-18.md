## Ranked ideas

### 1. When no candidate fits the counts, choose by multi-step error (built: `combined_misfit`)

- **Targets:** the realistic gap (item 1 above), without touching the tests.
- **Evidence:** on counts test cells the BIC winner fits the changes to their noise: the dispersion
  z-score of its standardized residuals (mean z² - 1 over its standard error) is at most 2.7 at the
  99th percentile, and its Ljung-Box whiteness z-score at most 1.7 at the 90th. The exceptions are
  canonical 102, 105 and 108, which no candidate represents. On the realistic runs the BIC winner's
  dispersion z has a median of 9.3, and 48 of 62 runs fail one of the two tests at z > 5. No
  candidate is right there, and one-step likelihood under a wrong model class rewards what is wrong
  in a way one step does not show. In system identification the answer is to select by
  simulation (free-run) error. On the stored candidates, realistic skill@20 goes from 40.7% to 49.9%, with no test cell lost or won (table above).
- **References:** Piroddi and Spinelli (2003), "An identification algorithm for polynomial NARX models
  based on simulation error minimization", *Int. J. Control* 76(17):1767-1781 (one-step error picks
  wrong structures; simulation error picks compact, right ones). Farina and Piroddi (2011),
  *Int. J. Systems Science* 43(2):319-333 (iterated multi-step predictors as a cheaper stand-in).
  Ribeiro and Aguirre (2018), "Parallel training considered harmful?", *Neurocomputing* 316:222-231.
  Ljung and Box (1978) for the whiteness test. Bergmeir, Hyndman and Koo (2018), *CSDA* 120:70-83
  (one-step and cross-validated scores are trustworthy only when residuals are uncorrelated, which is
  why the switch is gated on whiteness).
- **Risk:** the thresholds were checked on the realistic runs themselves, so they are not tuned
  blind. z = 5 is a conventional strong-evidence level, and the result is the same at z = 4 to 6.
  z = 4 loses 027 and 086 counts-short on the tests, so the margin there is thin. Selecting purely by
  in-data multi-step error was chosen on principle (factor 1); 1.25 does better on the realistic runs
  and 1.5 worse, which looks like noise, so it is not used.

### 2. Make the chosen model leaner: change places at a modified-BIC price, backward elimination of flows (built: `combined_lean`)

- **Targets:** strict-tier failures from extra parts (item 2).
- **Evidence:** replaying combined's stored models through the pass (`tests/study_combined_r2.py lean`,
  `cp` 1.5, `fp` 1.0): passes go from 678 to 681 of 912 test cells, and passes with no extra complexity (a proxy for strict) from 623 to 661. The changes in status: 6 gained (080 bias-short and meas-short, 083 bias-short from equivalent, 084 bias-long and meas-short, 088#3 meas-long), 3 lost to equivalent (008, 046#8 and 082 counts-short). Without the flow pass (change places only), 16 more passes
  have no extra complexity and no status changes. Ungated on the realistic runs (`ONLY=sd- ... lean OUT --real`) it changed 35 of 62 and cost skill: mean skill@20 over those 35 fell from 26.4% to 20.9%. Where no candidate is right, the pieces stand in for dynamics no model has. So it is gated: it runs only where the model fits
  the counts (idea 1's test), which also makes the complexity price meaningful.
- **References:** Zhang and Siegmund (2007), "A modified Bayes information criterion with applications to
  the analysis of comparative genomic hybridization data", *Biometrics* 63(1):22-32. BIC's conditions
  do not hold for change points, and mBIC charges about 3/2 log n per change. Its segment-length term
  is left out here; it is not a short-segment penalty. Killick, Fearnhead and Eckley (2012), PELT,
  *JASA* 107:1590-1598. Frick, Munk and Sieling (2014), SMUCE, *JRSS B* 76:495-580: the fewest change
  points that pass a multiscale test, with the chance of overestimating their number bounded.
  Fryzlewicz (2014), *Ann. Statist.* 42:2243-2281, the strengthened Schwarz criterion.
- **Known loss:** at BIC's price, flow elimination drops a true weak link in short counting runs (008,
  046#8 and 082 counts-short, pass -> equivalent).

### 3. A piece-fair runaway guard (not built; small, cheap, for combined)

A candidate is set aside only by one with **no more inflow change places** that behaves 1.25x better
in-data. The in-data multi-step error replays each model's own pieces, so a model with more pieces
can never be beaten unfairly by one with fewer. On the stored candidates it gives 43.5% realistic (from 40.7%) and loses no test cell. It changes
only sd-webshop-stock short. Idea 1 covers that run, so this guard adds nothing on top of it.

### 4. Stability selection for weak flows (not built)

Keep a flow only if a block-bootstrap refit selects it in most resamples (Meinshausen and Bühlmann
2010, *JRSS B* 72:417-473; Fasel et al. 2022, ensemble SINDy, *Proc. R. Soc. A* 478:20210904). The
block bootstrap sees the autocorrelation that the one-step likelihood ignores. It would replace idea
2's flow pass where BIC drops true weak links (008/046/082 counts-short). It needs refits per
resample, roughly 20-50x idea 2's flow pass.

### 5. A discrete (Poisson/Skellam) likelihood for small counts

Already queued in item 2f (056-sweep-zero-floor). Karlis and Ntzoufras (2006), *Stat. Med.* 25:1885-1905.

### Tried in the study and rejected

- **Prequential / frozen-inflow judge** (from each start the inflow pieces are held at their value
  then; Dawid 1984; Tashman 2000). It takes away the advantage of replaying pieces, but on the
  realistic runs it does not separate the models: forecasting the unannounced inflows dominates
  every candidate's error. On the stored candidates: 39.4% realistic at 2x (40.7% without it).
- **Cross-fed behaviour** (each candidate's dynamics fed every other candidate's outside inflows; a
  sound model should not run away under plausible inflows). It flags the runaway lna models
  (sd-hidden-chain long 24x, sd-population long 509x), but how an inflow is split depends on how it is
  written. A goal gap holds its inflow inside the gap flow, so it gets a double inflow when fed
  another candidate's constant. On the stored candidates it loses 21 test cells at 4x, mostly goal gaps (011, 047), plus floors (024), effects (017, 018), a hidden stock (015) and 048.
- **Linear stability (spectral radius of the one-step map).** The runaway realistic models are not
  linearly unstable (ρ ≤ 1 in almost every run, as for the good ones). They fail on how the outside
  inflow is attributed: in sd-webshop-stock, lna's model writes about 21 of the true 60 orders a day
  as a constant and the rest as an inflow reading the backlog itself (0.234 x backlog). When the
  grader feeds the truth's 60, the self-reinforcing inflow drives the backlog up.
- **Merging a constant inflow and a link into one `c + k x` flow** (054's form). It is the same
  equation, but the grader replaces only a found *constant* outside inflow with the truth's. A merged
  `c + k x` flow into a stock the truth feeds keeps its `c`, which is then counted twice. On the
  replay this took skill@20 to between -4 and -68 on 009, 010, 046 and 055 (each run clipped at
  -100% on the board). Kept in `prune.merge_inflows`, off. A scorer question for the user: should
  a found `c + k x` inflow have its `c` replaced like a constant?
- **Stacking / Bayesian model averaging** (Hoeting et al. 1999; Yao, Vehtari, Simpson and Gelman
  2018, *Bayesian Analysis* 13:917-1007). Stacking beats BMA when every candidate is wrong, but the
  ladder grades one model, and averaging flows across structures does not give one. Not tried.

## For combined's round 2 (queue item 2f)

Ladder results (936 paired test cells and 62 realistic runs; details in the strategy documents):

| | tests pass | strict | noisy pass / strict (110) | realistic skill@20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| combined (stored, stale) | 679 | 618 (66.0%) | 26 / 19 | 40.6% |
| lna | 579 | 432 (46.2%) | 20 / 12 | 17.5% |
| combined_misfit (idea 1) | 678 | 617 (65.9%) | 26 / 19 | 45.2% |
| combined_lean (idea 2) | 682 | 649 (69.3%) | 32 / 27 | 39.4% |
| combined_r2 (both) | 682 | 649 (69.3%) | 32 / 27 | 44.1% |

Put into combined:

1. **The misfit switch.** With counts, if the BIC winner fails dispersion or Ljung-Box whiteness at
   z > 5, choose the lowest in-data multi-step error among the candidates the 4x guard keeps.
2. **The gated leaner pass.** Change places at 1.5 log T; backward flow elimination with the
   behaviour check made both with the model's own pieces and with the pieces averaged; run only where
   the model fits the counts (|dispersion| ≤ 5, whiteness ≤ 5).
3. **Keep the 4x runaway guard.**

Both are small: about 40 lines of selection logic and a ~250-line post-pass, with no new specialist.
Not settled: the realistic gain on the ladder (+2 to +6 points paired) is smaller than in the offline
study (+9), and it is not significant on 62 runs. combined's specialists run under time caps, which
makes run-to-run noise on the loaded machine comparable to the effect. Re-measure combined, combined_misfit and combined_r2
in one session once the machine is quieter.
