A machine does actual work: a program, an AI agent or a person. Orchestra knows one by its events on the bus, and links its work to the model's flows only by a guess from timing.
more about this pageOne that runs as several copies is one machine with instances: events from baker/1 are instance 1 of the machine baker. What it publishes, its instances and the hand-offs between machines are observed on the bus; a machine on another system's bus is known by the counts a source reads there (what it produced into or consumed from a channel, marked counted); how it is engaged, what it listens for, what kind of thing it is, where its log is and which package it belongs to are what the machine announces about itself when it starts. A package may list a machine before it is first seen (marked unseen); what is observed always wins. What measures the system is not a machine: scripts, scraped URLs and sources are on the Instruments page. How a machine's work relates to the model's flows is not known in advance; each card shows the flows discovery infers it may be associated with, as guesses from timing with their confidence, and the Dynamics page shows them on the model.
A program becomes a machine by publishing to the bus: it announces itself with orchestra.machine.started (docs/machines.md).
